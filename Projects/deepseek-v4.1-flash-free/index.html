The story

Tihar begins in the sky —
and ends at your door.

For five days, as the year turns dark and cold, we are asked to make room for light. First the birds are fed. Then the guardians are honoured. Then the mothers. Then the goddess. And on the last day, the people we love are marked with colour and promised that they will never face the year alone.

Day one Kag Tihar

First, the messengers

Before a single lamp is lit, we feed the crows. They are the first to wake, the first to carry news across the valley. To honour them is to admit a quiet truth: we are all connected by what we pass on. A kind word travels farther than we think.

Tap each crow to offer a grain of rice.

Three crows wait on the wire. They seem to know something.

Day two Kukur Tihar

Then, the guardians

On the second day, dogs are garlanded and marked with tika. Not because they are gods, but because they have already been something better: loyal without condition. The dog at your door has guarded your sleep, your secrets, your bad days. Today, you guard its dignity.

Tap the dog to place a marigold garland.

The dog sits at the gate, tail sweeping the dust.

A guardian deserves flowers

Day three Gai Tihar & Laxmi Puja

The mothers,
and the lamps

The cow is honoured as a mother — for milk, for patience, for the quiet work of keeping a household alive. By evening the house is washed, the threshold is decorated, and lamps are set at every door. Laxmi does not enter a house that is only rich. She enters where someone has bothered to make room — with light, with cleanliness, with care.

Light all five diyos. Each flame is a welcome.

Five diyos wait in the dark. The mandala is holding its breath.

Day five Bhai Tika

Last, the promise

On the final day, sisters draw a seven-coloured tika on their brothers' foreheads and tie a thread of protection around their wrists. The old story says Yamuna kept her brother Yama waiting until the tika was complete — and death, for once, was patient. The meaning is simpler: I will stand in the way of anything that comes for you. Brothers return the blessing. Chosen family counts too.

Tap the portrait to receive the tika and the blessing.

A sister's hand hovers, seven colours on her fingertips.
